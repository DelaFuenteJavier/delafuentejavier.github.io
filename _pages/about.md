I hold a BSc in Microbiology (2013, Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona – UAB), an MSc in Biotechnology (2016, Universidad Autónoma de Madrid – UAM), and a PhD in Microbiology (2023, Universidad Complutense de Madrid– UCM). My research focuses on understanding the ecological and evolutionary factors that enable bacteria to adapt to diverse environments, with a particular emphasis on antimicrobial resistance and global warming.

In 2018, I joined Álvaro San Millán’s lab at the University Hospital Ramón y Cajal, and later at the National Center for Biotechnology (CNB-CSIC), where I studied the transmission and evolution of plasmid-mediated antibiotic resistance in clinical enterobacteria. In 2023, I earned a PhD with honors in Microbiology and began my postdoctoral work through a collaboration between San Millán’s lab and the Sánchez lab. During this time, I focused on identifying genomic patterns that could help predict the emergence of clinically relevant plasmid–bacteria associations. In 2025, I joined Macarena Toll-Riera’s lab at the Institute of Evolutionary Biology (IBE–UPF–CSIC). In Toll-Riera’s lab, I focus on exploring how bacteria respond to thermal perturbations, supported by a Juan de la Cierva fellowship.
